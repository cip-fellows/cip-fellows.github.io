About the CIP Fellows Team, including PIs and Co-PIs.

Current Team Members

Mary Thomas (PI)

  • Ph.D.
  • Computational Data Scientist, HPC Trainer
  • SDSC

Mary works at the San Diego Supercomputer Center in the Data-Enabled Scientific Computing Division, where she is the HPC/CI Advanced Computing Training Lead, and is responsible for developing and organizing educational and training materials to support the work of scientists running computational problems on HPC systems. Her research is focussed on computational science, parallel programming, advanced computational environments that support high-end scientific applications, and workforce development of the community of people who need to use HPC/CI resources. Thomas is PI or Co-PI of several NSF grants (2230127, 2017767, 2320977, 2346701, 2320934, 2112606, 1928224, 2404323), many of which focus on CyberTraining. Mary also works with graduate and undergraduate students through the SDSC HPC Students program. Her support and mentoring includes: mentoring interns in parallel computing on the Expanse cluster; developing advanced web technologies for the SDSC Advanced Computing Training program; and advising the IEEE Supercomputing Club,where she has sponsored several teams to participate in the annual Student Cluster Competitions at the annual Supercomputing conference. Mary is also a Research Faculty member at the Computational Sciences Research Center and the Department of Computer Science at San Diego State University. For more information, see https://www.sdsc.edu/~mthomas/index.html.

Marty Kandes (Co-PI)

  • Ph.D.
  • Computational and Data Science Research Specialist
  • SDSC

Marty Kandes is a Computational and Data Science Research Specialist in the High-Performance Computing User Services Group at SDSC. He currently helps manage user support for Comet — SDSC’s largest supercomputer. Marty obtained his Ph.D. in Computational Science in 2015 from the Computational Science Research Center at San Diego State University, where his research focused on studying quantum systems in rotating frames of reference through the use of numerical simulation. He also holds an M.S. in Physics from San Diego State University and B.S. degrees in both Applied Mathematics and Physics from the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor. His current research interests include problems in Bayesian statistics, combinatorial optimization, nonlinear dynamical systems, and numerical partial differential equations.

Andreas Goetz (Co-PI)

  • Ph.D.
  • Associate Research Scientist
  • SDSC

Andreas Goetz is an associate research scientist at SDSC where he leads a research group in Quantum- and AI-Enabled Computational Chemistry, working at the intersection of chemistry, life sciences, and scientific computing. His research draws on quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, molecular dynamics, and machine learning approaches to enable simulations of complex molecular systems on massively parallel computer architectures. He is a contributing author to the ADF and QUICK quantum chemistry software and the AMBER software for biomolecular simulations, which are widely used in academic and industrial research. He is also co-founder of ATTMOS Inc., which develops technologies for computational drug discovery. Andreas enjoys training the next generation of scientists in software engineering and numerical simulation methods via lectures, workshops, and supervision of interns. He is author of over 80 scientific publications and editor of the book ‘Electronic structure calculations on graphics processing units’. Prior to joining SDSC in 2009 Andreas performed postdoctoral research at the VU University in Amsterdam and obtained his undergraduate and Ph.D. degrees in chemistry from the Friedrich-Alexander University in Erlangen, Germany.

Expertise

  • Quantum Chemistry

Paul Rodriquez (Co-PI)

  • Ph.D.
  • Computational Data Scientist
  • SDSC

Paul Rodriguez is a Computational Data Scientist at San Diego Supercomputer Center. He received his PhD in Cognitive Science at University of California, San Diego (UCSD). He spent several years doing research in computational and cognitive neuroscience, including neural network modeling, hippocampus modeling, and dynamical systems simulations. He also worked on statistical methods for analysis and predictions in fMRI data, including time series analysis, machine learning methods with multivariate data, often in the context of learning and memory experiments and theory. Since joining SDSC in 2009, he has been supporting HPC applications for diverse users in science and humanities, with emphasis on data mining, machine learning, and deep learning.

Michael Farley

  • Senior Personnel, TIDE Project
  • Chief Technology Research Officer
  • SDSU

Michael Farley is the Chief Technology Research Officer at San Diego State University, where he leads the Research & Cyberinfrastructure group. His work focuses on facilitating research and managing campus-wide networks, computing, and storage resources for research purposes. He is senior personnel on the NSF-funded Technology Infrastructure for Data Exploration (TIDE) project, OAC 2346701, which aims to expand access to computational resources across the California State University (CSU) system. With 23 years of experience in higher education IT, Michael has held both technical and managerial roles, overseeing various areas of information technology.

Cyd Burrows-Schilling (Co-PI)

  • 23rd year in higher education
  • Sr. Research Facilitator
  • UCSD

Cyd is the Director of Research IT Services at UC San Diego and a seasoned research computing and data (RCD) professional having dedicated 9 years to the field at UC San Diego and with nearly 23 years experience in higher education. She now focuses on supporting a team of talented technical systems integration engineers (SIEs) and research facilitators (RFs) who together support researchers on our campus, assisting them with technical solutions and integrations to move their research forward from the ideation phase through to project completion and publication. She participates in a variety of national-level organizations and committees including CaRCC, EDUCAUSE, and Campus Champions, and currently serves as Co-PI on 2 National Science Foundation (NSF) awards. Cyd holds a BA in Anthropology from the University of San Diego, and a MS in Geographic Information Science and Technology (GIST) from the University of Southern California.

Gerard Au

  • Co-PI
  • Chief Information Officer
  • CSUSB

Gerard Au serves as Chief Information Officer for California State University San Bernardino (CSUSB), overseeing all aspects of information technology system, including the high-performance computing team. Currently, he serves as Co-PI on the NSF-funded Technology Infrastructure for Data Exploration (TIDE) project (OAC 2346701) and senior personnel for the CyberTraining: CIP project (OAC 2230127).

He has spent his 24-year career in higher education organizations, with nearly 14 years supporting researchers at R1 Institutions. In his current role, he was instrumental in creating the first Science DMZ at CSUSB with support of the former Vice President and CIO. Prior to joining CSUSB, Gerard supported $41M of funded research from FY2010-13. Gerard earned his Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering from the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) and Master of Business Administration from California State University, San Bernardino (CSUSB).

Susan Rathbun (Co-PI)

  • Program Manager
  • SDSC

Susan is a Program Manager, leading the San Diego Supercomputer Center’s (SDSC) Program and Events activities across the Center, and also serves as a Senior Project Manager for the Cyberinfrastructure Professionals (CIP) program. Past Team Members

Robert Sinkovits (Co-PI)

  • Ph.D.
  • Director of Education and Training
  • SDSC

Robert leads the education and training efforts at the San Diego Supercomputer Center, where he also serves as the industry program liaison and Co-PI/PM for the Expanse supercomputer. He holds a Ph.D. in Physics and has been engaged in the development and optimization of high-performance computing applications for nearly 30 years. Robert has collaborated with researchers spanning many fields including physics, chemistry, astronomy, structural biology, finance, ecology, climate, immunology and the social sciences, always with an emphasis on making the most effective use of high-end computing resources. He is also the PI of the NSF-funded Space Use Ecology Gateway, co-PI of the Voyager supercomputer award, co-PI of several NSF training projects and former director of XSEDE’s Extended Collaborative Support Service (ECSS).
