# Overview of Key Activities & Accomplishments
I transitioned into my current role as a Research Software Engineer coming from a web development and system administration background in enterprise IT. Through this CIP training grant I have received advanced training in areas such as Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, data management and parallel computing. I have also gotten some experience with applying for ACCESS allocations. Complementing this training, I was paired with a CIP mentor who was available to discuss topics in more depth as well as point me to additional resources. In addition, I’ve formed professional relationships with the other CIP Fellows through attending in-person conferences.

With this training that I’ve received, I have been able to produce training materials, documentation and a technical talk. I created an intro GitHub repository and associated video recording to train faculty, researchers and students on the subjects of git, containers and JupyterHub. I used these introduction resources as the basis for a technical brown bag that was delivered to IT professionals at San Diego State University who support researchers. In addition, I have developed documentation instructing San Diego State University researchers on how to access and use the National Research Platform’s Nautilus Kubernetes cluster for access to GPUs. Most recently, I made significant contributions to a National Research Platform git repository for a container that runs Globus which enables the data management tool in a containerized environment.

# Challenges
Some of the challenges that I have encountered are finding additional training on my own, balancing my time, understanding my own progress, communication with other CIP fellows and keeping everything organized. The training outlined by the program constitutes a majority of the time spent below in the table of activities, but it falls short of the 50% time that we have allocated to being trained on cyberinfrastructure topics. I was able to find good resources for specific topics, but often the resources don’t fill the full time allotted. With that being said, I did also find it challenging to balance my time between training and my day-to-day duties. I often felt a lack of understanding of progress. Cyberinfrastructure is a vast field, so having some paths identified with topics and skills aligned to these paths would be beneficial to understand how one is progressing through the training. I think that having a chat platform for the fellows would be beneficial to quickly exchange information, training material, and ask questions or share notes on training topics. Lastly, I think a common learning platform would be helpful to keep training and progress tracking in a centralized location and would be beneficial for keeping everything organized.
