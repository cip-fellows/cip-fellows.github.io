# **Overview of Key Activities & Accomplishments**

My goals through the CIP Fellowship is to be able to learn new skills and give back to the community. My background is in business, artificial intelligence, and software development. Through the CIP training grant I have been able to upskill my understanding of high performance computing, access to national resources such as NDP & ACCESS, and software containerization. Some technical skills I have learned include containerizing software applications, using package management, and high performance computing optimizations. I have used these skills to provide consultation for researchers to get access to compute on the NSF-backed Technology Infrastructure for Data Exploration (TIDE) cluster at San Diego State University and on National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR). 

I have completed a number of consultations with researchers on using the NSF-backed Technology Infrastructure for Data Exploration (TIDE) cluster at San Diego State University. This includes researchers at the James Silberrad Brown AI Center, SDSU Climate Informatics Lab, and Anna Rosen’s Computation astrophysics lab. For these laboratories, I have provided consultation services, onboarding, as well as containerization of their software. For example, I used my training from Kubernetes and Docker to help containerize a [FourCastNet](https://hub.docker.com/repository/docker/henrylisdsu/nvidia-cuda-12.4/general), a climate forecasting model using Nvidia’s hardware, for the SDSU Climate Informatics Lab. I was able to troubleshoot the researcher’s issues with package compatibility with the TIDE hardware, creating a consistent environment for the researcher’s to complete their research. One of my main role at SDSU is helping professors with creating environments for their instruction, so these additional projects complement my role well and I have been able to facilitate access to more researchers.

I have been able to host tutorials and workshops through Zoom for the students and researchers working at James Silberrad Brown AI Center. I have used my learning from LLM and my experience in machine learning to help create LLM services hosted on TIDE. After creating these LLM services, I have done workshops over Zoom to demonstrate how they can do the same on our cluster using kubernetes. These students and researchers are from the computer science and robotics departments.

In addition to assisting in the TIDE project, I have also contributed to the NAIRR project through my facilitation of various NAIRR initiatives such as the PEARC24 Hackathon for students as well as the office hours for prospective and current award recipients for NAIRR.

# **Challenges**

I think a main challenge for me is that I am learning a lot on my job as it is closely related to Cyberinfrastructure. A lot of the learning is hard to document and difficult to describe as well. I think training happens naturally for me so the hours written in the list of activities do not reflect the amount of learning I am doing.

Another challenge for me has been getting organized and involved with the NAIRR pilot. The information is very disparate and it is difficult to keep up with the ongoing changes. I will try to attend all the office hour meetings to absorb as much information as I can with the ongoing project.

I think finding good training is difficult as well. Many trainings do not come with a hands on portion due to the nature of HPC and the various configurations are different. I will try to look for more examples on how to deploy containers on ACCESS so that I can work on a project in the future.
